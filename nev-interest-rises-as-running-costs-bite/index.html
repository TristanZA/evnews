NEV Interest Rises As Running Costs Bite

Tristan Wiggill June 2, 2026 NEV sales

South Africa’s new vehicle market delivered its strongest May performance in 13 years, with growing interest in new energy vehicles becoming a more visible feature of consumer behaviour as buyers weigh fuel prices, finance costs and long-term ownership value.

Figures released by naamsa | the Automotive Business Council, show aggregate domestic new vehicle sales rose to 51,071 units in May 2026, up 12.8% from 45,287 units a year earlier. Passenger vehicle sales increased 16.3% to 36,871 units, while light commercial vehicle sales edged up 2.5% to 11,251 units.

The headline numbers point to a market that remains resilient despite a tougher economic backdrop. naamsa said consumers were increasingly focused on affordability, financing costs, fuel efficiency, safety and ownership value. That shift is helping to lift interest in hybrid, plug-in hybrid and battery electric vehicles, even though NEVs still account for a small share of total vehicle sales.

The clearest sign of momentum came in naamsa’s comment that NEV sales for April 2026 gained 120% compared with the same period last year. While the organisation did not provide a May NEV unit total in its release, the signal from dealers is that enquiries are rising as fuel costs and higher interest rates reshape purchase decisions.

Buyers ask sharper questions about electric options

Ryan Seele, an executive member of the National Automobile Dealers Association (NADA), said showroom conversations were changing.

“We are seeing a noticeable increase in discussions around hybrid and electric vehicles. Consumers are asking more informed questions about fuel savings, operating costs and long-term ownership benefits. While new energy vehicles still account for a relatively small portion of overall sales, awareness and consideration levels have increased significantly,” Seele said.

That interest is being driven less by novelty and more by household economics. Fuel prices rose sharply during April and May, contributing to headline inflation of 4.0% year-on-year in April. The SA Reserve Bank then raised the repo rate by 25 basis points to 7.0%, lifting the prime lending rate to 10.50%.

For a market where most vehicles are financed, the higher cost of credit has placed monthly repayments under greater scrutiny. naamsa warned that higher fuel and finance costs create a dual affordability challenge, particularly for first-time buyers, small businesses and transport operators.

Brandon Cohen, national chairperson of NADA, said buyers were taking more time before committing. “Customers are spending more time evaluating options, comparing finance offers and considering total cost of ownership before committing to a purchase. Dealers are having to work harder to convert enquiries into sales, but the demand is there,” Cohen said.

This is the space in which NEVs may gain further traction. Hybrids offer a familiar ownership proposition with lower fuel consumption, while electric vehicles appeal to buyers able to manage charging at home or at work. Price remains a constraint, with dealers reporting strongest demand below R500,000.

For now, NEVs remain a developing part of the market rather than a volume driver. As South Africans become more cost-conscious, fuel-efficient and electrified products are moving from the margins of consideration into mainstream buyer conversations.
