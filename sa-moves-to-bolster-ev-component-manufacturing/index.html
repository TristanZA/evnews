SA Moves to Bolster EV Component Manufacturing

Tristan Wiggill May 26, 2026 Manufacturing

Trade, industry and competition minister, Parks Tau, has published draft amendments to the Automotive Production and Development Programme (APDP) that would extend government incentives to a broader range of materials used in electric vehicle (EV) manufacturing, with a particular focus on battery production.

The proposals, which are open for public comment, include increased production credits and customs rebates designed to encourage investment in high-value EV components on South African soil.

The changes build on the EV white paper published by the Department of Trade, Industry and Competition (DTIC) several years ago and represent the latest in a series of policy steps taken to develop a domestic new energy vehicle (NEV) ecosystem.

Renai Moothilal, CEO of the National Association of Automotive Component and Allied Manufacturers (NAACAM), welcomed the proposals. "It's a step in the right direction. It's definitely a positive move and building on what was outlined a couple of years ago in the EV white paper published by DTIC," he said.

Regional dimension

Moothilal noted that the incentives would apply across the Southern African Customs Union (SACU), giving the proposals a regional dimension that could have broader industrial significance. "It's applicable across SACU and in some way could give some real impetus to what we talk about when we say regional industrialisation," he said.

The draft amendments follow two earlier policy interventions. An additional cash incentive for NEV components was introduced two years ago, and last year's budget included a 150% accelerated depreciation allowance for electric vehicles produced in South Africa.

The China question

South Africa's efforts to grow its EV manufacturing base come against the backdrop of China's formidable dominance of the global EV market.

Chinese manufacturers, backed by close to two decades of targeted industrial policy and substantial state support, account for more than 60% of EVs sold globally, as well as a commanding share of key component production including batteries.

Moothilal acknowledged the scale of the challenge but argued that South Africa's approach of using tax-based incentives and customs rebates, rather than direct subsidies, was appropriate given the country's fiscal constraints.

He said the goal was to position South Africa and the wider region as investment destinations and technology partners for large Chinese and other international producers.

"The mineral advantages that the region has does give some credence to bringing on board some of the large Chinese component producers," he said, adding that new entrants preparing to enter the South African market were already exploring ways to localise high-value components.

Moothila said more could always be done, but expressed satisfaction that policy was moving forward. "It is good to see some of this coming across the line."
