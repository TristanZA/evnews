Global EV Sales Surge to 20 Million Units

Tristan Wiggill May 20, 2026 Industry Reports

The global electric vehicle revolution reached a significant milestone in 2025, with worldwide sales exceeding 20 million units for the first time, representing one in every four new cars sold, according to the latest Global EV Outlook 2026 released by the International Energy Agency.

The landmark achievement arrives at a pivotal moment, as the ongoing energy crisis sparked by conflict in the Middle East forces governments and consumers alike to reconsider their reliance on imported oil.

The remarkable 20% year-on-year growth in electric car sales continues a trajectory that began in 2021, with annual increases of approximately 3.5 million vehicles each year. This sustained momentum has brought the global electric car fleet to around 5% of all vehicles on the road, collectively displacing 1.2 million barrels of oil per day in 2025 alone.

Europe Leads Growth Surge as Policy Frameworks Tighten

Among major markets, Europe experienced the most dramatic transformation in 2025. Electric car sales surged by more than 30% to surpass 4 million units, pushing the market share to 28% of total sales. This acceleration followed years of stagnation and came in direct response to stricter European Union CO2 standards for cars and vans that entered a new phase requiring a 15% reduction in emissions from new sales compared to 2021 levels.

The policy shift prompted automakers to adjust their pricing strategies and introduce more affordable electric vehicle models to comply with the new emissions targets. European manufacturers responded by expanding their electric offerings while continuing to face growing competition from Chinese imports, which accounted for almost 60% of electric cars imported into the European Union in 2025.

China Maintains Dominance Despite Slower Growth

China retained its position as the world's largest electric vehicle market, with nearly 55% of all new cars sold being electric. More than 13 million electric cars found buyers in the country during 2025, accounting for six out of every ten electric cars sold globally. However, growth rates moderated compared to the explosive expansion of previous years, partly due to the temporary suspension of the government's trade-in scheme in several cities during July 2025.

The Chinese market demonstrated remarkable price competitiveness, with 70% of battery electric cars sold in 2025 already cheaper than the average conventional car. In the small car segment, electric vehicles have effectively displaced conventional cars entirely, creating a market dynamic that pushes electric cars toward exceeding 90% of total car sales by 2035 according to the IEA's projections.

China's manufacturing prowess remained unchallenged, accounting for nearly 75% of electric cars produced globally in 2025. The country produced almost 22 million electric cars, representing a more than 25% increase over the previous year. Intense domestic competition squeezed profit margins, pushing manufacturers to seek opportunities overseas. Chinese electric car exports doubled to a record high of over 2.5 million units in 2025, with more than 35% of all Chinese car exports being electric vehicles, up from 20% in 2024.

United States Market Faces Policy Turbulence

The United States electric vehicle market experienced relative stagnation in 2025, with the sales share remaining just below 10% of total car sales. The year concluded with a significant downturn in the final quarter following the end of federal EV tax credits, highlighting the continued importance of policy support in driving adoption. The contrast between the stable overall year and the sharp decline at year's end underscored the sensitivity of the market to fiscal incentives.

Imports to the United States fell slightly in 2025, with the largest share coming from Mexico. The tightening of access to advanced manufacturing production tax credits posed challenges for Korean and Japanese battery manufacturers heavily exposed to the US market, potentially reshaping the competitive landscape in coming years.

Emerging Markets Drive Unexpected Growth

Perhaps the most surprising development of 2025 came from emerging markets, where electric vehicle adoption accelerated at rates exceeding even the established markets. Southeast Asia witnessed annual sales more than double to reach nearly 20% market share, led by impressive growth in Vietnam, Indonesia, and Thailand. Latin American sales grew by 75%, with Brazil and Mexico leading the charge.

More than 100 countries recorded electric car sales growth in 2025, and in one-third of these nations, electric vehicles represented at least 10% of new car sales. The availability of affordable Chinese-made electric cars played a crucial role in this expansion, bringing down prices and making electric mobility accessible to markets that previously seemed years away from meaningful adoption.

In Thailand, electric car prices reached parity with conventional vehicles two years ago, while Indonesia saw its average price premium for electric cars decline from over 50% in 2024 to around 40% in 2025. Vietnam emerged as the only country in Southeast Asia with a sizable domestic EV manufacturer offering vehicles at prices comparable to conventional cars, positioning it to potentially reach an electric car sales share of over 80% by 2035.

Energy Crisis Amplifies Economic Benefits of Electric Driving

The closure of the Strait of Hormuz due to conflict in the Middle East triggered a surge in crude oil and petroleum product prices globally, fundamentally altering the economic calculus for vehicle buyers. The crisis brought energy security and oil import dependency into sharp focus for many nations, with the road transport sector representing close to half of total oil demand.

Electric vehicles, with their significantly higher energy efficiency compared to conventional cars, offered immediate relief from escalating fuel costs. Based on average oil prices in April 2026, the annual fuel cost savings associated with driving an electric vehicle in the European Union grew 35% compared to 2025 savings. For corporate fleets covering long distances, the running cost advantages multiplied several times beyond those enjoyed by general consumers.

The crisis prompted several countries in Southeast Asia to announce plans to expand or extend EV tax incentives as part of their response. Vietnam, already the largest EV market in the region, moved to solidify its position. The energy security benefits proved particularly compelling for oil-importing nations, with China leading the way by avoiding consumption of approximately 1 million barrels of oil per day through its electric vehicle fleet in 2025.

Electric Trucks Emerge as Major Growth Segment

While passenger vehicles dominated headlines, the electric truck market posted extraordinary growth in 2025. Global electric truck sales more than doubled compared to 2024, reaching 9% of all truck sales worldwide. China drove the vast majority of this expansion, with sales doubling for the second consecutive year. One in four trucks sold in China carried electric powertrains by year's end.

The total cost of ownership for electric trucks achieved competitiveness in China thanks to falling battery prices and lower operating costs, even though purchase prices remained two to three times higher than diesel equivalents. European projections suggest total cost of ownership parity for electric trucks will arrive by 2030, supporting continued growth. The European Union already hosts over 1,000 charging points dedicated exclusively to electric trucks, enabling long-distance electric trucking operations.

New electric truck producers from the machinery and heavy industry sectors captured market share in China, with nearly 30% of the Chinese electric truck market going to new entrants that lack conventional models in their product lines. Chinese electric truck sales came almost exclusively from Chinese manufacturers using Chinese batteries, with CATL supplying 80% of the total, reflecting a highly integrated domestic ecosystem.

Battery Technology and Manufacturing Reshape Industry

Battery cell production remained heavily concentrated in 2025, with China accounting for over 80% of global output and even higher shares of active materials production. Nearly all battery cells used worldwide came from companies headquartered in China, Korea, or Japan, though the market share of Chinese producers grew especially rapidly in the European Union, nearly doubling since 2023.

Despite lithium-ion battery manufacturing capacity growing faster in the European Union and United States than in China during the year, projections suggest China will remain the largest producer of batteries and battery materials through 2035 based on stated policies. Narrow profit margins put pressure on some battery manufacturers, creating an industry shake-out that may accelerate consolidation.

Battery technology continued advancing rapidly, with patents related to batteries accounting for nearly half of all energy sector patents. The first 1,000-volt models appeared in 2025, enabling charging times under 10 minutes in some cases. New power electronics materials, battery cell technologies, and pack architectures enabled more efficient, higher-voltage, and faster charging systems. The number of electric cars capable of using chargers above 250 kilowatts remained below 5% of the vehicle stock, but sales grew alongside the expansion of ultra-fast and megawatt-scale chargers.

Software and Artificial Intelligence Transform Vehicle Design

Following the lead of pure-play EV makers, most major automakers began developing vehicles with more centralized software systems allowing key functions and systems to be updated remotely. Battery electric vehicles emerged as the most advanced examples of these software-defined vehicles, which rely on more centralized control architectures enabling a wide range of developments supported by falling sensor prices, more powerful computing chips, and artificial intelligence applications.

Advanced driver assistance systems, including automated steering and speed control, represented key applications alongside EV battery management improvements. The deployment of autonomous vehicles accelerated, with driverless taxis, all of them electric, now operating commercially in more than 20 cities, mainly in China and the United States.

The greater number of semiconductors needed to support increasingly digital and autonomous vehicles created growing reliance on supply chains that are already geographically concentrated. Managing cybersecurity risks emerged as an increasingly important challenge for automakers as connectivity and automation expanded.

Charging Infrastructure Expands Globally

Private charging infrastructure reached an estimated 43 million light-duty vehicle charging points globally in 2025, supporting an electric vehicle stock of around 76 million. About one-third of private charging points existed in China, one-third in Europe, and one in six in the United States.

Home charging remained the preferred method for those with access, due to its relative affordability and convenience. Access varied significantly by housing type and region. In Norway, where 64% of the population lives in detached or semi-detached houses, around 90% of EV owners could charge at home. In China, where only 7% of residents live in single-family houses, approximately 50% of EV owners had home chargers, though another third accessed shared residential chargers.

Policy measures supporting home charging access included building regulations in many countries. The European Union revised its Energy Performance of Buildings Directive in 2024 to require new or renovated buildings to include pre-cabling for EV charging. Kenya's 2024 National Building Code mandated 5% of parking spaces in all new buildings be equipped for EV charging, while India's Model Building Bye-Laws called for charging infrastructure covering 20% of parking capacity.

Vehicle-to-Grid Technology Offers Grid Flexibility Potential

The first commercial offers for vehicle-to-grid services for private EV owners appeared in 2025, allowing electric vehicles to feed electricity back to the grid during peak demand periods. This technology promised to help manage grid capacity constraints as EV deployment and charging speeds increased. However, few vehicle-to-grid capable models were available, the regulatory landscape remained fragmented, and standards had not yet achieved clarity.

Smart charging, which reduces peak demand by shifting charging loads to off-peak hours, offered additional grid management opportunities. These measures gained importance as projections showed electricity demand from EVs could exceed 1,500 terawatt-hours by 2035, growing around sixfold from 2025 levels based on current policies.

Two and Three-Wheelers Continue Electrification Leadership

The most electrified road transport segment, two and three-wheelers, continued expanding in 2025. While sales in China and India, the world's largest two-wheeler markets, grew only slightly to a total of 8.4 million units, sales doubled in Vietnam, underpinning global growth. African sales grew markedly to reach about 70,000 two-wheelers in 2025, more than 80 times the volume at the decade's start.

Electric three-wheelers maintained a sales share of over 25%, continuing to increase even as the overall three-wheeler market contracted. These smaller vehicles offered particularly compelling economics for emerging markets, with lower purchase prices and operating costs making electric mobility accessible to a broader population.

2026 Forecast Points to Continued Acceleration

Global electric car sales are expected to grow to 23 million in 2026, representing 28% of total car sales. Europe appears poised for the largest growth among major markets, with sales projected to increase by around 20%, bringing the share to one in three cars sold. China's electric car sales are set to continue growing, albeit at a slower pace than previous years, to reach almost 60% of total car sales.

Sales across Asia Pacific countries other than China are expected to grow by over 50%, while Latin American sales are projected to rise by 45%. The wider economic impacts of the ongoing Middle East conflict might temper overall car sales, but in many regions, upside potential exists depending on how, when, and which policies are enacted in response to the energy crisis.

First quarter 2026 data revealed a complex picture. Global sales of around 3.9 million units came in 8% lower than the same period in 2025, primarily because of lower sales in China and the United States following key policy changes. However, this overall decline masked strong growth in many countries.

European sales climbed close to 30% year-on-year, countries in Asia Pacific excluding China saw year-on-year sales growth of 80%, and Latin American sales rose by 75%. Around 30 countries recorded record-breaking monthly sales in March 2026, with a further 60 countries posting year-on-year sales growth.

Preliminary April data showed monthly electric car sales in China growing to a record high of over 60% of total car sales, even as year-to-date figures remained lower than 2025 levels, suggesting a strong recovery from earlier slowdowns.

Long-Term Outlook Envisions Transport Transformation

Looking toward 2035, projections suggest the global fleet of electric vehicles will grow more than sixfold from 2025 levels to reach as many as 510 million units, excluding electric two and three-wheelers. The increasing cost-competitiveness of EVs, combined with tighter CO2 and fuel economy standards, will drive market growth, pushing the share of EVs in global car sales to around 50% by 2035 in the IEA's exploratory scenarios.

The share of conventional internal combustion engine cars continues shrinking in all scenarios, with sales never returning to their 2017 peak. In China, the trajectory points toward electric vehicles exceeding 90% of total car sales by 2035. European CO2 standards will continue driving sales, with the proposed Automotive Package potentially moderating the 2035 outlook while still seeing electric car sales shares exceed 90%.

Global annual oil displacement by electric vehicles is projected to triple to around 5 million barrels per day by 2030, with China accounting for 2.7 million barrels per day. By 2035, electric vehicles worldwide could displace approximately 9 million barrels of oil per day in the Current Policies Scenario, with China representing roughly half of the global total displacement. Growing deployment of electric trucks will contribute significantly, avoiding consumption of 1 million barrels per day by 2035.

Trade Dynamics Reshape Global Automotive Industry

Trade patterns continued evolving dramatically in 2025, with around one-quarter of electric cars produced being traded between countries. China overtook the European Union to become the largest car exporter in 2024, and the gap widened in 2025 as Chinese exports doubled. More than 35% of China's car exports were electric vehicles, up from 20% in 2024.

Imports from China accounted for 55% of electric car sales in 2025 in countries outside Europe and the United States, up from less than 5% just five years earlier. More than half of electric cars sold in Southeast Asia came from Chinese brands, while one-third came from a Vietnamese manufacturer. European Union electric car exports grew 25% in 2025, but imports also rose by around 35% year-on-year to more than 900,000 units, with China accounting for almost 60% of these imports.

In the first quarter of 2026, electric car exports from China more than doubled compared with the same period in 2025, offsetting weaker domestic sales. However, Chinese exports could face headwinds as inventories accumulated. Estimates suggest exports exceeded overseas sales by more than 25% in 2025, pointing to potential market adjustments ahead.

Policy Responses Will Shape Future Trajectory

The current high oil price environment draws consumer attention to the economic benefits of driving electric vehicles. Electric cars generally have lower running costs than internal combustion engine vehicles, mainly due to their higher efficiency, and the recent rise in oil prices resulting from the Middle East conflict has further increased the cost savings associated with driving an EV.

Oil-importing emerging and developing economies across the world have some of the strongest incentives to implement policies to further accelerate electric car adoption. These include countries in Southeast Asia such as Thailand, Latin America such as Chile, and Africa such as South Africa. In the short term, this could be achieved by increasing imports of affordable Chinese electric cars, while in the longer term, incentivizing domestic supply could offer an additional pathway.

The road transport sector's close to half share of total oil demand positions policy responses to the current crisis to shape the global car market for years to come. The oil crisis of the 1970s prompted introduction of fuel efficiency standards that nearly doubled the fuel economy of conventional cars between 1975 and today. During the COVID-19 pandemic, many countries introduced EV subsidies to boost uptake and support broader economic recovery. The current energy crisis appears poised to trigger a similar wave of policy innovation focused on accelerating the transition to electric mobility.

The transformation extends beyond passenger vehicles to encompass trucks, buses, and two and three-wheelers, creating a comprehensive shift in road transport. Combined with advancing battery technology, expanding charging infrastructure, growing manufacturing capacity, and evolving trade patterns, the electric vehicle revolution appears to have reached an inflection point where momentum becomes self-sustaining in an growing number of markets worldwide.

As the industry navigates challenges including supply chain concentration, cybersecurity risks, grid integration requirements, and evolving policy frameworks, the fundamental trajectory appears established. The question for most markets has shifted from whether electric vehicles will dominate to when that dominance will be achieved and how the transition will be managed to maximize economic, environmental, and energy security benefits.
