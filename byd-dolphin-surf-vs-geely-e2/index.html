BYD Dolphin Surf vs Geely E2

Tristan Wiggill May 30, 2026 Vehicle Comparisons

For years, battery-electric vehicles were largely premium products in South Africa, priced far beyond the reach of most private buyers. Now, two Chinese compact EVs have pushed the entry point to around the R340 000 mark: the BYD Dolphin Surf and the Geely E2.

The BYD Dolphin Surf arrived first and briefly held the title of South Africa’s cheapest electric car. The Geely E2 has since undercut it by a small margin, with local pricing from R339 900, compared with the Dolphin Surf Comfort at R341 900.

On paper, the Geely E2 looks like the more complete car: it is larger, more powerful, offers more range on the WLTP cycle, charges faster, and includes a generous warranty and service package.

But the BYD Dolphin Surf has its own strengths, especially for buyers who want a proven EV brand, a compact city-friendly footprint, and BYD’s well-known Blade Battery technology.

So, if you are a South African consumer considering your first EV, which one should you test drive first? Let’s compare them properly.

Pricing and model range

Both vehicles are offered in two derivatives in South Africa.

At entry level, there is almost nothing between them. The Geely E2 Aspire is only R2 000 cheaper than the BYD Dolphin Surf Comfort. At the upper end, the Geely E2 Apex is also slightly cheaper than the Dolphin Surf Dynamic.

In real-world buying terms, a R2 000 price gap is unlikely to make the decision on its own. Finance deals, dealer discounts, trade-in assistance, insurance quotes and availability may matter more. Still, the headline is clear: the Geely E2 is currently the cheaper car, but only just.

Winner on price: Geely E2, narrowly.

Size and practicality

This is one of the biggest differences between the two cars.

The Geely E2 is slightly larger. It is 210 mm longer, 85 mm wider, and has a 150 mm longer wheelbase. That should translate into better cabin space, especially for rear passengers.

The E2 also has a clear practicality advantage because Geely publishes a 375-litre boot capacity, expanding to 1 320 litres, plus a useful 70-litre front trunk. That frunk is handy for charging cables, shopping bags, laptop bags or small emergency items.

The BYD Dolphin Surf is more compact. That can be a benefit in dense city driving, narrow parking bays and tight apartment garages. But if this will be your only household car, the Geely looks more versatile.

Winner on practicality: Geely E2.

Battery, range and real-world usability

Driving range is one of the first questions South Africans ask about EVs, and rightly so. Public charging is improving, but most EV buyers will still want a car that can comfortably handle daily use, school runs, commuting, errands and the occasional longer trip.

This comparison needs a bit of explanation.

BYD’s product brochure prominently lists NEDC range figures of 300 km for the Comfort and 400 km for the Dynamic. However, NEDC is an older, more optimistic test cycle. The more realistic WLTP figures are 232 km for the Comfort and, based on local reporting, around 295 km for the Dynamic.

The Geely E2 product brochure lists a WLTP combined range of 325 km for both Aspire and Apex derivatives. That means the fairest real-world comparison is not BYD’s 400 km NEDC figure versus Geely’s 325 km WLTP figure. Instead, it is more reasonable to compare:

  • BYD Dolphin Surf Comfort: 232 km WLTP
  • BYD Dolphin Surf Dynamic: around 295 km WLTP
  • Geely E2: 325 km WLTP

On this basis, the Geely E2 offers the strongest claimed real-world range, especially considering that even the base Aspire gets the same battery and range as the higher-spec Apex.

The BYD counters with its Blade Battery, a major part of BYD’s global EV identity. BYD’s Blade Battery is widely regarded as one of the company’s key strengths, and the Dolphin Surf brochure makes a point of highlighting it.

But for a consumer comparing range per rand, the Geely has the advantage.

Winner on range: Geely E2.

Charging

Charging speed matters in two different ways. For home charging, most owners will plug in overnight, where ultra-fast charging is less important. For road trips or busy days, DC fast charging becomes more important.

The Geely E2 has a clear advantage in DC charging capability, with a claimed maximum DC charging power of 70 kW. The BYD Dolphin Surf is limited to 30 kW in Comfort form and 40 kW in Dynamic form.

This matters if you plan to rely on public fast chargers. A higher DC charge rate can mean shorter stops and less waiting, assuming the charger can deliver the required speed.

Both cars support V2L, or vehicle-to-load functionality, meaning the car can power external devices. That is especially useful in South Africa, where many buyers may appreciate the ability to run small appliances, camping gear, laptops or emergency equipment from the car’s battery.

Winner on charging: Geely E2.

Performance

Neither of these cars is designed to be a hot hatch. They are affordable compact EVs aimed at commuting, city driving and everyday use. But there is still a noticeable difference in performance.

The Geely E2 is substantially more powerful, with 85 kW compared with the BYD’s 55 kW. It also offers more torque and a quicker 0–100 km/h time.

For daily urban driving, both feel responsive enough because EVs deliver instant torque. But on highways, inclines, overtaking situations or when carrying passengers, the Geely’s extra output make it feel more relaxed.

The other interesting difference is drivetrain layout. The BYD Dolphin Surf is front-wheel drive, while the Geely E2 is rear-wheel drive. Rear-wheel drive is unusual at this price point and gives the E2 a more balanced driving feel, although most buyers will need a test drive to decide whether that matters to them.

Winner on performance: Geely E2.

Interior and technology

Both vehicles are modern inside, but they take slightly different approaches.

BYD Dolphin Surf standard features:

  • 10.1-inch intelligent touchscreen
  • 7-inch digital instrument panel
  • Apple CarPlay and Android Auto
  • Bluetooth phone connectivity and audio streaming
  • FM and AM radio
  • 4-speaker sound system
  • USB Type-A and Type-C ports
  • Synthetic leather seats
  • Steering-wheel-mounted controls
  • Wireless phone charging on Dynamic
  • Tilt and telescopic steering on Dynamic

Geely E2 standard features:

  • 14.6-inch HD touchscreen
  • 8.8-inch LCD instrument cluster
  • Flyme Auto infotainment system
  • Apple CarPlay and Android Auto
  • Bluetooth connectivity
  • Vehicle remote detection and control
  • Front and rear USB ports
  • 4-speaker audio on Aspire
  • 6-speaker audio on Apex
  • Wireless charging on Apex
  • 256-colour ambient lighting on Apex
  • Vegan leather seats
  • Front centre armrest with cupholders

The Geely’s cabin is more advanced on paper. Its 14.6-inch touchscreen is larger than the BYD’s 10.1-inch unit, and the 8.8-inch instrument display is also larger than the BYD’s 7-inch screen.

The BYD’s interior still looks modern and simple, and the rotating-style BYD touchscreen interface used in other BYD models is a familiar brand signature. But from features alone, the Geely E2 offers the more premium tech experience.

Winner on interior tech: Geely E2.

Safety and driver assistance

BYD Dolphin Surf standard safety features:

  • Front airbags
  • Side airbags on Dynamic
  • Side curtain airbags
  • Seat-belt reminders front and rear
  • ISOFIX child-seat anchors
  • Tyre-pressure monitoring system
  • Reverse camera
  • Rear parking sensors
  • Electronic Stability Control
  • ABS
  • Traction Control System
  • Electronic Brake-force Distribution
  • Hill-start Assist Control
  • Hydraulic Brake Assist
  • Auto Hold
  • Electric Parking Brake
  • Cruise Control

Geely E2 standard safety features:

  • Driver, passenger, front side and side-curtain airbags
  • Front and rear seat belts with pretensioners and force limiters
  • Occupant detection system for all seats
  • Tyre-pressure warning
  • Rear parking radars
  • Rear-view camera
  • 360-degree panoramic view with transparent chassis view
  • Adaptive Cruise Control
  • Automatic Emergency Braking
  • Lane Departure Warning
  • Forward Collision Warning
  • Intelligent High Beam Control
  • Lane Change Assist
  • Door Open Warning
  • Rear Collision Warning
  • Rear Cross Traffic Alert
  • Blind-spot Detection
  • Electronic Stability Control
  • ABS and Brake Assist
  • Hill-start Assist and Hill Descent Control

The Geely E2 clearly offers a broader driver-assistance package, particularly on the higher-spec Apex. The inclusion of features such as Adaptive Cruise Control, AEB, Blind-spot Detection, Rear Cross Traffic Alert and 360-degree camera visibility makes it feel closer to a higher segment.

The BYD Dolphin Surf is not poorly equipped from a safety perspective, especially for a compact entry-level EV, but the Geely’s ADAS list is more comprehensive.

Winner on safety and driver assistance: Geely E2.

Warranty, service plan and ownership cover

Ownership support is especially important for first-time EV buyers. South African consumers will want reassurance around battery life, after-sales support, servicing and roadside assistance.

The Geely E2 brochure makes a strong ownership case. It lists:

  • 4-year / 150 000 km vehicle warranty
  • 8-year / 200 000 km battery warranty
  • 5-year / 100 000 km service plan
  • 5-year unlimited roadside assistance
  • Wallbox charger included, subject to terms and conditions and Geely Finance approval

That is a very strong package at this price point.

BYD’s battery warranty is competitive, and BYD has strong global credibility in EVs. However, based on the available brochure and local market reports, Geely’s overall warranty and service-plan package looks more generous.

Winner on ownership cover: Geely E2.

Brand confidence and dealer support

This is where the comparison becomes less one-sided.

BYD has become one of the world’s most important EV manufacturers. It has deep experience in battery technology, electric drivetrains and large-scale EV production. In South Africa, BYD has also been building momentum, recording strong local EV sales, with the Dolphin Surf playing an important role.

Geely is also a major global automotive group, but the E2 is newer to the South African market. Its value proposition is excellent, but buyers may want to ask practical questions about:

  • dealer coverage in their province
  • parts availability
  • service booking times
  • roadside support
  • software updates
  • resale-value expectations

While the Geely E2 looks very strong on paper, BYD benefits from stronger local EV momentum and brand recognition in South Africa’s emerging EV market.

For buyers who are nervous about EV ownership, BYD’s established EV reputation may carry weight. For buyers who are comfortable with Geely and have a nearby dealer, the E2’s spec advantage will be hard to ignore.

Winner on EV brand confidence: BYD Dolphin Surf.
Winner on value-backed ownership package: Geely E2.

Design and positioning

The BYD Dolphin Surf looks like a compact urban EV. It is small, cheerful and youthful, with a strong city-car personality. Its brochure leans heavily into lifestyle imagery, colour choices and smart features.

The Geely E2 feels more mature and substantial. It has crossover-like proportions, more width, a longer wheelbase and a more conventional “main car” feel. 

In simple terms:

  • The BYD Dolphin Surf feels like a clever compact city EV.
  • The Geely E2 feels like a larger, better-equipped small family EV.

Which one looks better will come down to personal taste, but their positioning is quite different.

BYD Dolphin Surf: key strengths and weaknesses

Strengths

  • Strong BYD EV brand credibility
  • Blade Battery technology
  • Compact and easy to park
  • Stylish, youthful design
  • Good urban EV package
  • Competitive entry price
  • Apple CarPlay and Android Auto
  • V2L capability
  • 8-year / 200 000 km battery warranty

Weaknesses

  • Less powerful than the Geely E2
  • Shorter WLTP range
  • Slower DC charging
  • Smaller body and likely less cabin/boot space
  • Fewer advanced driver-assistance features
  • Higher-spec Dynamic is close in price to the better-equipped Geely E2 Apex

Geely E2: key strengths and weaknesses

Strengths

  • Currently the cheaper entry point
  • Larger and more practical
  • 375-litre boot plus 70-litre frunk
  • Stronger 85 kW motor
  • Rear-wheel-drive layout
  • 325 km WLTP range
  • Faster 70 kW DC charging
  • More advanced safety and ADAS package
  • Larger 14.6-inch touchscreen
  • Generous warranty, service plan and roadside assistance
  • Wallbox charger included, subject to terms and conditions

Weaknesses

  • Newer nameplate in South Africa
  • Dealer footprint and resale confidence may still need to prove themselves
  • Brand may not yet have BYD’s EV recognition among local buyers
  • Long-term ownership experience in SA is less established

Which one should you test drive first?

Choose the BYD Dolphin Surf if:

You mainly drive in the city, want the smallest and easiest car to park, and place a lot of value on BYD’s EV and battery reputation. The Dolphin Surf is also appealing if you like its styling and want a simple, friendly first EV from a brand that is already strongly associated with electric vehicles.

It makes the most sense as a second household car, city commuter, student car, urban runabout or daily work-and-school transport.

Choose the Geely E2 if:

You want the most car for the money. The E2 is larger, more powerful, has better claimed WLTP range, faster DC charging, more boot space, a frunk, more advanced safety tech and a stronger ownership package.

It makes more sense if this will be your only car, if you regularly carry passengers, or if you want an affordable EV that feels less like a city car and more like a proper small family vehicle.

Verdict: the Geely E2 wins on paper, but the BYD still has a case

If this comparison is based purely on the numbers, the Geely E2 is the better-value EV. It is cheaper at entry level, larger, faster, more powerful, longer-legged, quicker to charge and more generously equipped. Its warranty and service-plan package also gives it a strong ownership-cost argument.

The BYD Dolphin Surf, however, should not be dismissed. BYD is a major EV brand with strong battery credibility, and the Surf is a compact, likeable and city-friendly electric car. For buyers who want the reassurance of BYD’s EV expertise, the Dolphin Surf remains a compelling option.

But for most South African consumers comparing these two before booking a test drive, the recommendation is clear:

Test drive the Geely E2 first if you want the best overall value. Test drive the BYD Dolphin Surf first if brand trust, compact size and BYD’s battery reputation matter more to you than outright specification.

Either way, the bigger story is positive: South Africans finally have two credible new EVs at around R340 000 — and that could be the start of a much more accessible electric-car market.
