EV Buyers Chase Calm, Not Speed

Tristan Wiggill May 12, 2026 White Papers

A new global white paper from lubricants giant Castrol has revealed something that may surprise the motoring world: the single biggest draw of electric vehicles is not performance, not cost savings, and not environmental credentials. It is peace and quiet.

The report, titled The Quiet Electric Car Revolution, surveyed 3,000 drivers across the United States, Germany and China, and found that tranquillity has emerged as the defining emotional benefit of EV ownership.

The quiet driving experience ranked as the number-one functional feature that prospective EV buyers are aware of, ahead of instant acceleration and automatic software updates. A remarkable 81% of EV considerers identified it as a top appeal feature, while 83% of existing owners said they were satisfied with it.

Dirk, a 58-year-old driver from Hamburg who owns a VW ID.7, put it simply: "What I like most about my EV is actually the silent driving. It's like floating. I'm past the age where everything needs to go vroom vroom vroom."

For South African drivers navigating the daily grind of urban congestion, pothole stress and long commutes, this finding carries particular resonance. The idea of a car as a sanctuary rather than a source of frustration is a compelling one.

Performance That Puts Drivers in Control

Beyond the calm, the research found that EV drivers feel a heightened sense of control behind the wheel. A full 84% of owners said they enjoy the feeling of agile cornering and responsive handling, while 78% believe the EV driving experience is superior to that of petrol, diesel or hybrid vehicles.

Elaine Borseth, President of the EV Association of the US, described it well: "The power, the acceleration, the smoothness, the quietness. It's a superior driving experience. It's different and so much better."

Importantly, drivers are not looking for technology to replace their control. They want it to enhance it. The research found that 80% of respondents agree their car is one of the few places where they feel completely in control, and smart driver-assist features are valued precisely because they support that feeling rather than undermine it.

More Than a Car

The Castrol study also found that EVs are increasingly seen as expressions of identity. Some 74% of respondents said their car is an important part of who they are, and 44% said their vehicle reflects their passion for new technology.

EVs are now associated with being future-proof by 74% of respondents, a figure that leaves hybrids at 50% and traditional ICE vehicles trailing at just 34%.

Inside the cabin, the picture is equally compelling. With no engine taking up space, EV interiors offer more room, better design and features such as ambient lighting and massage seats. Some 83% of EV owners report satisfaction with cabin comfort, and 73% view their car as a multipurpose space for work, entertainment and relaxation.

The road ahead for EV's still has its challenges. Range anxiety, charging infrastructure and upfront costs remain real barriers. But if Castrol's research tells us anything, it is that the emotional case for going electric is already being won, one quiet, gliding journey at a time.
