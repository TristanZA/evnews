Affordable hybrids and EVs gain traction 

Tristan Wiggill May 13, 2026 EV Adoption

 

South Africa’s new energy vehicle (NEV) market is showing fresh signs of momentum as cheaper models and a widening choice of brands bring battery electric vehicles and plug in hybrids closer to the mainstream.

In a recent CNBC Africa Shifting Gears discussion, Fulufhelo Mandane, Executive: Strategic Partnerships at Absa, said Chinese vehicle makers have become a major force in local sales, with Chinese brands reaching about 13.6% of the total market in April 2026 (equivalent to 6,546 units).

He explained that the shift is being reinforced by household budget pressure, which is pushing car buyers toward sharper pricing and higher specifications.

“Consumers know they’ve got options,” noted fellow panelist Thembinkosi Pantsi, vice chairperson of the National Automobile Dealerships Association (NADA).

Pantsi noted that dealerships are responding through multi franchise showrooms that carry traditional brands alongside newer Asian entrants, helping retailers sustain volumes - and jobs - during a difficult economic period.

EV entry prices pull adoption forward

Mandane linked the rise in NEV interest to improving affordability at the entry level and greater visibility of new brands. He pointed to a lower price point for a newly introduced battery electric model as a catalyst for improving EV sales.

“Just look at March, where we saw the introduction of the Geely E2 at around R340,000,” he pointed out. He added that the (similarly priced) BYD Dolphin Surf supported about 232 units sold in March 2026, a meaningful number in a market where battery electric adoption has previously been slow.

Growth in plug in hybrids is also emerging as a practical bridge for consumers still wary of charging constraints. Mandane stated that PHEV sales rose by almost 498% year-on-year, moving from 111 units to about 664 units in March. Dealers say this matches a wider pattern in which buyers still value familiar driving habits while looking for relief from volatile fuel costs.

Mandane revealed that pressure at the pumps is influencing buying decisions. “We are seeing a lot more queries going out in the sales space for new energy vehicles,” he said, adding that finance demand is starting to follow.

Policy, skills and finance set the pace

Industry participants say the next phase [of NEV adoption] will depend on whether South Africa can expand the enabling ecosystem quickly enough. That includes charging infrastructure, technical skills for servicing high voltage drivetrains, and policy certainty that supports investment.

Pantsi said the retail industry is preparing for a different operating model as NEV sales grow. “The dealer of the future is going to be different to the dealer of today,” he stated, referring to NADA’s Motoring Mavericks initiative aimed at building skills for the next generation of technicians and dealership staff.

Banks are also reviewing product design as NEVs change the cost structure of ownership. Mandane intimated that vehicle finance may increasingly need to account for complementary equipment at home and at work.

“We are exploring different ways in which we can look at supporting the industry,” he said, citing items such as charging stations and inverters as part of the ownership journey.

For now, South Africa's NEV story is being written at the intersection of price, confidence and support. As more brands deepen their dealer networks and consider local assembly, the next five years are likely to show whether the country's NEV market can move from early adoption to mass market reality.
