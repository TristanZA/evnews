SA Explores Tax Incentive to Boost EV Uptake

Tristan Wiggill May 20, 2026 taxation

A new study could pave the way for a salary sacrifice-style scheme that makes EVs more affordable for South African workers

The African Climate Foundation (ACF), working in partnership with the Just Energy Transition Project Management Unit (JET PMU) in The Presidency, has issued a call for proposals to conduct a rapid diagnostic evaluation of a potential tax incentive mechanism aimed at accelerating the uptake of new energy vehicles (NEVs) in South Africa.

The request for proposals, published under reference RFP-12-JET IP, invites qualified consultants and research consortia to assess whether South Africa's existing tax framework could be adapted to make electric vehicles significantly more accessible to private consumers — without placing additional strain on the national fiscus.

The Challenge Facing South Africa

South Africa's electric vehicle market has been slow to grow. High upfront purchase prices, combined with tight government finances, have made it difficult to introduce the kind of direct cash subsidies that have driven global EV adoption.

The transport sector currently accounts for approximately 14 per cent of the country's total greenhouse gas emissions, and the slow transition to cleaner vehicles is seen as a meaningful obstacle to meeting South Africa's climate commitments under the Just Energy Transition framework.

The ACF and JET PMU believe that an innovative, fiscally responsible policy lever may already exist within South Africa's tax legislation — and that it simply needs to be properly examined and designed.

Looking to the United Kingdom for Inspiration

The study draws inspiration from the United Kingdom's salary sacrifice scheme for zero-emission vehicles, a programme that has proven highly effective in accelerating EV adoption among employed workers.

Under such a scheme, employees agree to sacrifice a portion of their pre-tax salary in exchange for a benefit-in-kind — in this case, access to an electric vehicle. The arrangement reduces the employee's taxable income, lowers the overall cost of the vehicle, and provides employers with an attractive staff benefit at the same time.

South Africa already operates similar tax structures in other areas, including pension contributions and medical aid deductions, as well as allowances for the personal use of company vehicles. The diagnostic study will investigate whether these existing frameworks could be extended or adapted to support NEV uptake in a comparable way.

Norway is also cited as a relevant example, where company car allowances have played a significant role in driving widespread EV adoption across the population.

What the Study Will Cover

The successful service provider will be expected to deliver a comprehensive body of work over a 17-week period, running from June to September 2026. The assignment will cover five main areas of investigation.

The first phase will involve a comparative review of international EV tax incentive models, examining how schemes in countries such as the United Kingdom and Norway have been structured, what tax rates and vehicle value caps have been applied, and what lessons can be drawn for the South African context.

The second phase will assess South Africa's existing legislative and regulatory framework, including the Income Tax Act, the Tax Administration Act, and the Labour Relations Act, to identify which provisions would support or hinder the introduction of such a scheme, and what amendments would be required.

The third and most substantial phase will involve the development of an econometric and fiscal model to simulate the potential impacts of different scheme designs. This will include projections for government revenue, consumer savings across different income brackets, projected increases in NEV sales, and broader developmental outcomes such as job creation, infrastructure investment, and reductions in carbon emissions.

The fourth phase will bring together key stakeholders — including National Treasury, the South African Revenue Service, the Department of Trade, Industry and Competition, the National Association of Automobile Manufacturers of South Africa, leasing companies, and organised labour — to validate the findings and refine the proposed implementation roadmap.

The fifth and final phase will produce a summary report and presentation for the steering committee, which will be chaired by the JET NEV PMO and will include representatives from National Treasury, the Department of Transport, and the Presidency's Policy and Coordination Committee.

A Focus on Inclusion and Gender Equity

The evaluation has a strong emphasis on gender responsiveness and social inclusion. The study must assess how different scheme designs would affect women, youth, and persons with disabilities in different ways, and must apply an intersectional lens to understand how overlapping identities influence access to and benefit from any proposed incentive. The evaluation is required to achieve a Gender Responsive rating on the DPME Gender Results Effectiveness Scale.

Who Should Apply

The ACF is seeking proposals from teams that bring together expertise across tax law, labour law, economic and fiscal modelling, public policy, the automotive sector, stakeholder facilitation, and gender analysis. Proposals may be submitted by individual organisations or by consortia, provided that the full range of required skills is represented within a single submission.

Proposals must be submitted through the ACF portal and must include a technical proposal of no more than 12 pages, a signed conflict of interest declaration, a tax clearance certificate, company registration documents, and a completed pricing template. Submissions via email will not be accepted.

The deadline for queries is 25 May 2026 at 17h00 South African Standard Time. The deadline for proposal submissions is 1 June 2026 at 17h00 South African Standard Time.

Technical queries may be directed to Christel Jacob at the JET PMU via christel.jacob@jetpmu.org.za, with the subject line referencing RFP 12. Portal assistance is available through grants@africanclimatefoundation.org.

A Pivotal Moment for South Africa's EV Sector

If the diagnostic study finds that a salary sacrifice-style scheme is feasible and well-designed, it could represent a turning point for South Africa's electric vehicle market.

A well-structured tax incentive would lower the barrier to entry for thousands of employed South Africans, stimulate demand for locally manufactured NEVs, support the growth of the domestic automotive value chain, and contribute meaningfully to the country's decarbonisation goals — all without requiring direct government expenditure.

The findings of the study will be presented to the Interministerial Committee as an evidence-based proposal, with the potential to inform future tax amendments and policy frameworks that could reshape the trajectory of EV adoption in South Africa.

Proposals close 1 June 2026. For more information, visit the ACF portal under RFP-12-JET IP.
