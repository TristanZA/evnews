Bolt EV fleet comes to Cape Town

Tristan Wiggill May 18, 2026 Ride Hailing

Bolt has struck a partnership with China’s Dongfeng Motor Group to introduce electric vehicles on its ride hailing platform, starting with a fleet in Cape Town.

The deal brings Dongfeng’s Box hatchback and a higher spec 007 sedan to Bolt riders, with fleet operations handled by Yugo Rides. Bolt says the move is aimed at improving ride economics for drivers as fuel prices rise and operating costs remain a pressure point.

Simo Kalajdzic, who oversees Bolt’s South African operations, said the country sits at the centre of the firm’s local expansion plans. “With the investment we are making, and the platform we are building, South Africa remains a strong strategic priority for us.”

Cape Town pilot and fleet models

Cape Town has been selected as the first launch market because charging options are more developed there than in many other South African cities, and because the company plans to roll out the vehicles in stages.

“We are taking a phased approach to launching this fleet as we consider the current infrastructure limitations, including the need for enough charging stations,” Kalajdzic said.

Kalajdzic added that the company has invested about $180 million in the South African market and claims more than half of the country’s ride hailing business. That scale gives Bolt a test bed for new vehicle programmes and a route to place electric cars into high mileage service.

For Dongfeng, the partnership offers a visible entry point in a market where Chinese carmakers are increasing exports. The arrangement also puts unfamiliar models in front of riders.

Costs, charging and long term strategy

Electric vehicles can reduce day to day running costs for ride hailing drivers, particularly when petrol and diesel prices move higher. The impact of the Iran war on oil prices has added urgency for ride-sharing platforms that rely on drivers absorbing fuel costs.

However, public charging infrastructure remains limited outside major hubs, and grid constraints have shaped energy planning for businesses. Bolt’s staged approach suggests a focus on operational reliability, with fleet management designed to simplify charging, maintenance, and vehicle availability.

The announcement also lands as Bolt weighs longer term capital markets options. The company, backed by Sequoia Capital, has expanded across more than 50 countries and 850 cities and previously raised €628 million in a 2022 funding round that valued it at €7.4 billion.

Kalajdzic said Bolt “will consider options, when market conditions are right,” a comment likely to keep speculation about an initial public offering in focus. Bolt runs food delivery and scooters and says South Africa sits among its ten biggest markets worldwide.

Industry analysts will watch the Cape Town rollout for evidence that electric fleets can scale in South Africa. If utilisation rates hold and charging downtime stays low, the model could support expansion to other cities and offer a template for similar launches across the continent.
